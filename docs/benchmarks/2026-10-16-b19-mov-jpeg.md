## 3. (b) JPEG: packJPG against lepton

Every JPEG goes to the store route, where lepton packs it (B2). Precomp has
its own JPEG recoder, packJPG (`-t` lists "J = JPG"), so on the Extreme and
Insane chains it is the challenger. The incumbent in that slot is the store
route with lepton, in every profile.

### What lepton refuses, from lepton itself

Its own error messages (lepton-jpeg-python 0.5.8), on the declined files:
`Unsupported4Colors: doesn't support 4 color channels`; `UnsupportedJpeg:
image dimensions larger than 16386x16386`; and for the 7 small web files,
scan-structure quirks (`progress can't have two DC first stages`,
`non optimal eobruns not supported`). The limits, probed with synthetic
images (Pillow):

| image | lepton |
|---|---|
| RGB 16,386 × 8 | accepted |
| RGB 16,387 × 8 | refused |
| RGB 8 × 16,387 | refused |
| grayscale | accepted |
| CMYK | refused |

So **4 components, or either side over 16,386 px**, both visible in the
JPEG's frame header. (The old comment in `jpeg.pack` said "wider than
16384 px"; it is 16,386, on either axis.) Those two causes were **143.9 of
the 144.1 MB** lepton declined across the four corpora. The scan quirks can't be read from the
frame header; those files stay stored, as before.

**The frame header is not near the start.** In every CMYK file it sat
**565 KB** in, behind a print ICC profile (17 segments). The analyzer's
64 KiB head misses all 13, so `jpeg.frame_header` walks the marker chain,
seeking over each segment. Phone photos had it within 80,775 B, web images
within 28,314 B.

**How common.** A header scan of all 6,686 files named `.jpg`/`.jpeg`: 53
are not JPEGs at all (43 PNGs, 6 WebPs, 4 macOS `._` forks), and **17 are
refused by header**, 143.9 MB: the 14 design files and 3 others. Rare
outside print work, and heavy when present.

## 7. (d) and (e): two estimator bugs older than B19

### (d) The container overhead was under-booked

Found by checking B19's estimates against real archives. A store-only
estimate is a zero-width range ("exact"), yet 64 Takeout MOVs came out
**3,173 B bigger** than it, on `main` too. `_overhead` added each file's
*basename* once, piped files included. A stored file's zip entry carries its
full archive name (`stored/<root>/<path>`) in both the local header and the
central directory, uncompressed. On a synthetic 4-deep tree of 12 files the
archive was 2,748 B over. The disk preflight books this figure, so it may err
high, never low.

The analyzer now records each file's archive-name length
(`FileInfo.arc_name_bytes`, from `archive_name()`, which a test pins to
`engine._collect`) in `analyze_tree`, `analyze_tree_safe` and the GUI's own
analysis walk. Through the engine, the estimate now sits **19–76 B per file
above** the real archive on four tree shapes (1×3, 4×12, 2×64, 6×200), where
it was up to ~230 B per file below.

### (e) Calibration wrote a ratio into a slope

J7 learns from every finished GUI job. `_calibrate` passed the job's
`final_bytes / orig_bytes` (stored bytes included) as `achieved_factor`, and
`record()` blended it into `Rates.factor_for`. Since B8 that value is the
*slope* k in `(1 − k) + k·r`, not a ratio; B8's docstring says it was
"renamed in spirit but not in name", and calibration was never updated. The
skeptic's measurement, five jobs into a temp store, then a 100 MB text file at
probe 0.4:

| store | 7-Zip k | estimate | Precomp+7-Zip k | estimate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| shipped | 0.726 | 22.58 MB | 0.769 | 21.54 MB |
| after jobs at ratio 0.97 | 0.912 | 18.11 MB | 0.922 | 17.87 MB |
| after jobs at ratio 0.20 | 0.325 | 32.20 MB | 0.335 | 31.96 MB |

It runs backwards: jobs that compressed *better* made the estimate *more
pessimistic*, and incompressible ones made it optimistic, enough to push
Silesia's Precomp ceiling (0.1826) under the measured 0.2195. **This
machine's own store held k = 0.4126 for 7-Zip**, three jobs that each
achieved about 0.18, which made every Normal estimate of compressible data
**33% pessimistic** (22.58 → 30.10 MB on the probe-0.4 file). The test
`test_the_achieved_ratio_is_learned_too` checked that the stored number
approached 0.20 and never looked at the estimate.

**Fix:** the ratio is no longer learned, and stored factors (every one a
ratio) are ignored. Rates are still learned. A slope can't be recovered
from a whole-job ratio without the probe readings behind it, so this waits
for a design that records them. Also: a job whose piped bytes are at least
a quarter Precomp-recoded media teaches no rate (one CMYK job would have
moved the learned Extreme rate by −23%); below that, `recode_cost`'s
modelled seconds are taken out first.

## 8. Things worth not rediscovering

- **A JPEG's frame header can sit half a megabyte in.** Print files carry
  their ICC profile ahead of it. Never trust a fixed-size head for JPEG
  structure; walk the markers.
- **Files named `.jpg` aren't all JPEGs.** 53 of 6,686 here were PNG, WebP
  or macOS forks. Route and price by content where the engine does.
- **Lepton has no effort setting, so it does not distinguish the profiles.**
  Anything that ranks profiles has to treat its bytes as the same in every
  row, or it will recommend the slowest one for nothing.
- **Render the page.** Both GUI defects in (c) passed every test.
- **A worktree has no `tools\bin`.** It's untracked, so a run from the
  branch's worktree finds no zpaqfranz, flac, qpdf or balrogg, and Insane
  silently runs as Extreme. Junction it: `mklink /J <worktree>\tools\bin
  <main>\tools\bin` (gitignored).
- **Check a test asserts the outcome, not the mechanism.** The calibration
  test confirmed the stored factor moved and never looked at the estimate it
  produced, and so it pinned a backwards feedback loop in place for five
  weeks.
- **Under a ceiling, every term must be a worst case.** B18 learned it for
  MP3; B19's lepton term and B13's Ogg term both used means inside a "≤ X"
  until real wallpapers came out 96 KB over.
