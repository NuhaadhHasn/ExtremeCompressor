# 2026-10-16: B20, zip-structured containers that Extreme stored by mistake

**Machine:** Dell Inspiron 15-3567, i5-7200U (2 cores / 4 threads), 15.9 GB
(`Win32_Processor` checked). **Every timing below is contended** (other
sessions, WSL's `vmmem` busy); every benchmark process ran at below-normal
priority. Sizes and restore results don't depend on contention. **No time
here is a pin.** The owner had paused heavy work, so B20 was measured on
small samples (18.8 MB): enough to show the effect and set a ceiling,
not a corpus-scale benchmark.

Harnesses: `C:\excmp-bench\b20\` — `zip_perfile.py` (`setup`, `run`),
`run_e2e.cmd` (B19's `b19_app.py`, `main` against the branch via
`EXCMP_REPO`). Logs: `perfile-*.jsonl`, `e2e\`. File names from the owner's
data are not recorded here.

---

## 1. The defect

B5 (2026-09-07) built `analyzer.probe_expandable`: it opens a zip's central
directory and answers "Precomp can expand this" when a member is deflated and
none is encrypted. **Only the estimate used it.** The planner kept piping by a
suffix list, `_PRECOMP_EXPANDABLE` (`.zip .gz .jar .apk .docx .xlsx .pptx
.pdf`). So:

- `.epub`, `.odt`, `.ods`, `.odp` were probed (`expandable = True`) and then
  **stored at Extreme with "high-entropy data … recompression would gain
  ~0%"**, because their deflated bytes look random (a realistic synthetic
  EPUB measured 7.99 bits/byte).
- Zip-structured files under other names (Android AAR and AAB, NuGet, APPX,
  VSIX, wheels, WAR, CBZ) **were never probed at all**.

On the owner's drives (a read-only listing, `zipfamily.tsv`): 31 EPUB
(326 MB), 102 AAR (114 MB), 2 AAB (121 MB), 10 NuGet (42 MB), 14 ODT/ODS
(37 MB), 6 APPX (26 MB), and a WAR and a wheel. About 670 MB.

| APPX | 2 | 6,015,103 | **47.35%** | 0.49–0.71 | all stored |

## 4. The estimate

**Size.** The newly routed containers are priced at `_ZIP_CONTAINER_RATIO =
(0.36, 0.89, 1.0)`. On a Precomp chain `expected` is shown as a ceiling
("≤ X"), so it is the worst file of the 22 (an EPUB at 0.888). B5's 0.55,
fitted on PDF/Office, would have sat under every EPUB measured. `low` is the
best file (a NuGet at 0.361). B5's own types (`.pdf .docx .xlsx .pptx`, and
`.zip .jar .apk` that the suffix list always piped) keep B5's figures, so
their estimates don't move. Against the archives above: **all five inside
the range, all five under the ceiling.**

**Time.** Expanding every member is Precomp's slow regime. Compress phases
(from the app's own events): EPUB 0.26, NuGet 0.40, AAR 0.52, APPX 0.79
MB/s, median **0.46**; self-test 0.59–1.63, median **1.5**, against the
generic chain's 1.41 and 6.5. Before the fix EPUB's 31.7 s sat far above a
13.8 s ceiling, and AAR and NuGet just above theirs. The containers join
B19's `recode_cost` as kind `"zip"`, after which all four measured runs sit
inside the range. (The 64 KB ODT job is process start-up, 1.9 s against
0.1 s estimated; the model has no fixed per-job cost, an old gap that only
shows on tiny jobs.)

## 5. The review

One reviewer (correctness and estimate honesty together), every finding
reproduced before its fix:

| | finding | evidence | fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **a zip-headed file could fail analysis, and so the job, on every profile** | `zipfile` raises `NotImplementedError` for a "version needed" above 6.3 (7-Zip reads it fine): 288 of 20,000 single-byte mutations of a small zip escaped. Already true for `.zip` names; B20 widened it to every zip head | the probe catches everything (K3: analysis never fails a job) |
| 2 | **"any member deflated" was too weak** | the app's own `.excmp` (stored payload + deflated manifest) and a zip of deflated random data were piped and priced at ≤ 0.891; real ratio 1.002. At 3 GB it would have recommended Extreme | a tenth of the file must be members Precomp can work on (below) |
| 3 | temp under-booked | `.pcf` over input: EPUB 18.2× byte-weighted (9.4–96.7× per file), ODT/ODS 10.4× (6.3–16.5×), against the preflight's 5× default; for the owner's 326 MB of EPUBs, ~2.6 GB booked against ~6.9 GB needed | `.epub` 20×, `.odt/.ods/.odp` 12× in `_INFLATION_BY_EXT` |
| 4 | a low-entropy container lost its estimate | an `.xpi` of stored text: was 0.137–0.313 from its own samples (actual 0.225), became 0.36–1.0 | the worst-file ratio applies only to containers that look random |
| 4′ | **B5's low-entropy branch inverted its range** (older) | it returned expected 0.55 beside a high of probe × 1.05: inverted whenever the probe read under ~0.52 | B5's figures now cap the sample-based ones instead of replacing them; B5's measured files (probe 0.88–0.91) get exactly what they did |
| 5 | a corrupt end record could make the probe read the whole file | 4 MB file, one 3,999,978-byte read | a central directory past 32 MiB, or past the file, is refused unread |
| 6 | "19–57%" were group totals | the worst file saved 11.2% | the reason says 11–64% per file |

**The criterion for #2, measured rather than guessed.** Counting only
deflate output that actually compressed (`compress_size` under 95% of the
member's size) separated the bad cases, but on B5's own real files
(`Programming Tutorials`, 38 Office documents) it would have dropped **22
of 38**: their bulk is images, which Precomp works on anyway (it expands a
PNG's own deflate and recodes JPEGs with packJPG, stored in the zip or not).
Counting PNG/JPEG members too keeps **all 38** and all 22 B20 samples
(lowest real share 0.63), while the `.excmp` payload and deflated random data
still count for nothing. Threshold: a tenth of the file.

After the fixes, 416 tests across the related suites pass, and the
estimator backtest passes with the corpus guard clean.

## 6. What changed

- `analyzer.probe_expandable` also probes any file whose head is a zip local
  header (`PK\x03\x04`), whatever its name, and `_zip_is_expandable` needs a
  tenth of the file in members Precomp can work on; it never raises and
  never reads more than a sane central directory.
- `planner.plan` pipes whatever the probe calls expandable on a chain with
  Precomp, beside the suffix list (which still carries `.gz`, a single
  deflate stream the zip probe can't see). `_INFLATION_BY_EXT` books EPUB
  and OpenDocument temp space.
- `store_reason`: on Fast/Normal such a file now says Extreme expands it
  (measured 11–64% smaller per file), not "~0%".
- `estimate`: `_ZIP_CONTAINER_RATIO` for containers that look random,
  `"zip"` in `_RECODE_RATE`, and B5's low-entropy branch no longer inverts.

Unchanged: an encrypted zip, or one with no deflated member, is not
expandable, and stays stored unless the suffix list pipes it (B5's 263 MB
`pass=123.zip` case).

Tests: `tests/test_b20_zip_family.py` (51).
