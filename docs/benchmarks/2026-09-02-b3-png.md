# Benchmark: PNG (B3) — and why the plan's answer would have broken the product

- Machine: Nuhaadh-Laptop, i7-3540M (2C/4T), 16 GB, threads=2
- Corpus: **15 real PNGs, 7,148,769 bytes** from `My Graphical Projects` and `Pictures`
- Date: 2026-09-02

## The plan was wrong, and measurably so

Roadmap B3 said: *"PNG specialist: `oxipng -o max` default, ECT `-9` under
Extreme."*

**oxipng does not preserve bytes.** On 10 real PNGs it saved **31.79%** and
produced **0 byte-identical files**. It rewrites the image to a smaller
encoding that renders identically — pixel-lossless, not byte-lossless.

Our guarantee is a byte-identical restore checked by SHA-256. An oxipng'd file
fails its own ledger entry. The D9 gate would catch that and refuse to
publish, so the archive would simply never be written — but the *dangerous*
version of this bug is somebody "fixing" it by recording the new hash, at
which point the user silently gets back a different file from the one they put
in. That is the single failure this product exists to make impossible.

**oxipng belongs in Phase C**, beside the AV1 re-encoder: opt-in, clearly
labelled, changes your file.

## And the lossless route is simply better

PNG is deflate all the way down, which is exactly what Precomp expands and
reconstructs exactly.

| approach | result | byte-identical |
|---|---|---|
| stored (what shipped before) | −0.11% (container overhead) | ✅ |
| oxipng | −31.79% | ❌ **0 of 10 files** |
| **precomp → 7z (Extreme)** | **−38.41%** | ✅ **15/15** |

**The lossless path beats the lossy one on ratio.** That is the whole result.

| profile | in | out | saved | time | verified |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| normal | 7,148,769 | 7,156,861 | −0.11% | 1.0 s | 15/15 |
| extreme | 7,148,769 | **4,402,850** | **38.41%** | 32.7 s | 15/15 |

Normal is unchanged and correct — it has no Precomp, so there is nothing to
expand and piping PNGs would only cost time.

## Why PNGs were being stored

The planner exempts `_MEDIA` from the Precomp override, on the sound reasoning
that a JPEG's or an MP4's entropy is *real* and expanding it is pointless.
**PNG is the exception in that set**: its high entropy is deflate output, not
inherent randomness. One entry — `_DEFLATE_MEDIA = {".png"}` — and the media
guard stops applying to it. JPEG deliberately stays out: its entropy is real,
and it has its own specialist on the stored route (B2/lepton).

## The bug this uncovered, which was worth more than B3

Routing PNGs through Precomp **broke extraction**:

```
ContainerError: stage 'sevenzip' produced 317 MiB, far beyond the 64 MiB
implied by the manifest ledger - refusing to continue
```

PNG inflates through Precomp by about **50×** (a 3.5 MB tar became a 177 MB
`.pcf`), because expanding deflate yields raw pixels. The decompression-bomb
guard allowed a flat 8× over the ledger total. Extraction runs the chain
backwards, so 7-Zip legitimately produces that huge intermediate before
Precomp restores it — and the guard, correctly by its own logic, refused.

**No multiplier can fix this.** One loose enough for PNG's honest 50× is no
defence at all; one tight enough to be a defence rejects PNG.

**So the guard is now exact instead of heuristic.** `StageRecord.input_bytes`
records what each stage actually *consumed* at compress time. On extraction
that is precisely what the stage should produce, so the bound is a number we
measured rather than a guess — the same move D0.3 made when it bounded
extraction by the ledger instead of by a ratio. `input_bytes: 0` means "not
recorded" and falls back to the old multiplier, so archives written before the
field existed still extract (there is a test that strips the field and
restores such an archive).

This makes the bomb defence **stronger**, not weaker: a chain that used to be
allowed 8× the ledger total is now held to 1.25× a figure the writer measured.

## The other consequence: temp space

`_PRECOMP_INFLATION` was a flat 5×. For a PNG folder that under-reserves temp
by an order of magnitude, and running out of temp mid-pipeline is the exact
failure the J4 preflight exists to prevent. `precomp_inflation()` is now
per-type and **byte-weighted**, so a folder that is 1% PNG does not reserve as
though it were all PNG — a preflight that refuses jobs which would have
succeeded gets switched off, and then it protects nobody.

**The honest cost:** Extreme on PNGs took 32.7 s against Normal's 1.0 s, and
peaked at ~177 MB of temp for 7 MB of input. Worth it for 38% on a folder that
previously compressed by nothing, but it is not free and the preflight now
says so.
