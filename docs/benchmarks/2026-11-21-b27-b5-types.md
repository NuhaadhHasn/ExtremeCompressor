# 2026-11-21: B27, B5's six types at Insane, and B28, archives inside archives

**Machine:** Dell Inspiron 15-3567, i5-7200U (2 cores / 4 threads), 15.9 GB, shared with other
Claude sessions. Every row ran `engine.compress` (D9 included) then `engine.extract`, below-normal
priority, one process at a time, with an independent SHA-256 of every restored file
(`C:\excmp-bench\b21\b21_app.py` driving `b13_app.py`, on main's code, frozen while it ran).
**12 rows on 10 corpora: 0 mismatches.** Times are the unbiased clock
(`QueryUnbiasedInterruptTime`) with the sleep that the old harness counted reported separately as
`slept_s`. Corpora are copies of the owner's own files, from `C:\excmp-bench\b27\corpora\`
(backed up from the trash before it was emptied); the contention log is
`C:\excmp-bench\contention.tsv`.

The question: **B5's types (`.docx .xlsx .pptx .zip .jar .apk`) still ran at the chain's rate
at Insane, while B26 priced the same kind of container (AAB, AAR, NuGet, APPX, EPUB, ODT) by what
Precomp expands it to. Is the container model better for them too?**

## 2. The B5 types: the chain stays

Measured over expected, each model, one row per corpus:

| corpus | chain model | container model (`_PRECOMP_EXPANDABLE` emptied) |
|---|---|---|
| xlsx | 0.89x, inside | **0.29x, below its low** |
| pptx | 1.64x, inside | 1.41x, inside |
| zip | 0.89x, inside | 0.83x, inside |
| jar | 1.48x, inside | 2.14x, inside |
| **docx** | **2.84x, past its high (3,567 s)** | **0.97x, inside** |
| apk | 0.51x, **below its low** (safe side) | 0.58x, **below its low** (safe side) |

**Neither model beats the other.** The chain is inside its range on xlsx, pptx, zip and jar, past
its high on docx (the unsafe side: the estimate is too short) and below its low on apk (the safe
side). The container model is inside on docx, pptx, zip and jar, and below its low on xlsx and apk
(the safe side). Its expected is 2.1x under the measured on jar (the unsafe side, though inside the
range). **Four of six each**, and the one case where either leaves its range on the unsafe side is
the chain's, on docx: 4,632 s against an expected of 1,629 s and a high of 3,567 s, where the
container model (4,751 s expected) matches. That is **one corpus**, so by the project's rule
(*two corpora, or don't quote the first*) it was **not** acted on in B27 (acted on in B29, last section): no suffix left
`_PRECOMP_EXPANDABLE` and the estimates were unchanged. A second `.docx` corpus (one round) is the
next session's first job; if it agrees, lift `.docx`, and read `.pptx` again (1.64x on the chain,
1.41x on the container model, both inside).

**Why docx, read off the probe (no new run).** The docx corpus is **PNG by bytes**: its central
directories hold 71.7 MB of PNG members in 80 MB (plus 20.3 MB deflated and 5.7 MB JPEG), and one file,
`office-docx-020` (36.9 MB, 33.7 MB of PNG), is 46% of it. The chain model has no idea a document is
mostly screenshots; the container model prices PNG members at B22's PNG rate, which B26 validated on
the ODT, EPUB and APPX. So the docx miss is the same mechanism as B26's, not a docx-specific effect.
The caution against lifting by suffix is the other corpora: **xlsx** reads 6.1 MB of PNG in a 7.2 MB
corpus and the container model overshoots it 3.4x, and the **pptx** corpus is mostly JPEG (24.0 of 40.0 MB).
That points at deciding by **content** (what the probe counts), not by suffix, as the next session's
design question: the counts are already in `FileInfo`.

Savings, for the record: docx 44.1%, xlsx 71.2%, pptx 52.5%, zip 69.4%, jar 62.6%, apk 70.2%.

## 3. B28: a member that is itself an archive

A `.war` holds `.jar` files, and Precomp expands the streams inside those too, so a nested byte
costs several plain deflated ones. B26's model priced `lib/*.jar` as plain deflated and the `.war`
corpus ran past its high. The probe now counts nested members (`.jar .war .ear .zip .aar .apk
.nupkg .whl .vsix` by name) apart, and the estimator charges each `_NESTED_FACTOR` plain deflated
bytes (`excmp/estimate.py`).

Fitted against the plain rate (5.38 s per MB, compress + self-test), fastest of two rounds:

| corpus | nested bytes | measured | implied factor | at 3.0: low / expected / high |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `.war` (3 files, 99% nested jars) | 18,026,265 | 300 s (r1 512 s slept) | 3.05 | 195 / 294 / 1,173 |
| synthetic nested zip (one 10.1 MB file) | 16,226,377 | 242 s (r1 266 s) | 2.75 | 175 / 264 / 1,053 |

**`_NESTED_FACTOR = 3.0`**, inside its range on both, expected within 1.3x of the measured on both
(`tests/test_b28_nested_archives.py` holds both). The first commit had **5.3**, fitted on the
`.war`'s round 1 alone, which the machine slept through; a reviewer found that the war test passed
for any factor from 2 to 7.9, and the second corpus then said 2.75.

What it does not reach: **`.zip`, `.jar` and `.apk` keep the chain's rate**, because
`_recode_kind` returns nothing for the B5 suffixes. The nested rule fires for `.war .ear .aar
.nupkg .whl .vsix` and for zips under any other name. (The reviewer's repro: the same bytes
estimate 95/143/572 s as `x.war` and 60/96/213 s as `x.zip`.) Of the corpora here only the `.war`
and the synthetic one are affected; B26's six corpora and the jar, nupkg, aab and apk corpora
carry no nested bytes or are unchanged, and the estimate for every one of them is identical on main
and on this branch.

A review also found a **crafted central directory amplified by the new factor**: a 226-byte
`.war` claiming a 900 kB `lib/b.jar` was estimated at 26,465 s. A zip's members cannot occupy more
than the zip, so the claimed compressed sizes are now clamped to the file and scaled down when
they add up to more.

## 4. What was not measured

- A second `.docx` corpus (section 2), a second round of every large corpus, and any `.whl` or
  `.vsix`. The nested factor rests on two corpora, one of them synthetic.
- The jar corpus ran 1,510 s against 1,018 s expected on the chain (698 on the container model)
  though only 1% of it is nested: part of the nested factor's gap may be a general "class-file
  archives cost more" effect, which the 1% jar corpus cannot separate.

## B29 (2026-11-23): a second `.docx` corpus, and `.docx` priced by content at Insane

**Corpus:** 55 `.docx` files, 43,759,565 B, copied byte for byte from the trash backup (not in B27's set; the
manifest is `C:\excmp-bench\temp-copies\trash-new\manifest.tsv`). The probe reads 32.1 MB of PNG (73% of the
bytes), 6.5 MB of JPEG and 34.6 MB of deflated members. Insane, one round, quiet machine (`slept_s` 0.0),
the same harness as B27: **1,794 s compress+D9, 788 s extract, 45.5% saved, 55 of 55 restored byte-identical,
no independent mismatches.**

| corpus | MB | PNG by bytes | measured | chain model (low/exp/high) | container model (low/exp/high) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| docx (B27) | 80.0 | 90% | 4,632 s | 1,023 / 1,629 / 3,567 (**2.84x, past its high**) | 2,198 / 4,751 / 19,002 (**0.97x**) |
| docx2 (B29) | 43.8 | 73% | 1,794 s | 560 / 892 / 1,954 (**2.01x, at its high**) | 1,096 / 2,295 / 9,172 (**0.78x**) |

Two independent corpora agree: the chain model is 2x-2.8x too short on the unsafe side, the container model is
0.78x-0.97x of the measured time (slightly long, the safe side) and holds both inside its range. So **a `.docx`
is priced by its counts at Insane when it is PNG-dominant** (`zip_png_bytes >= 0.5 * size`, `_DOCX_PNG_SHARE`).

What the review of the change reproduced, and what was done:

- **A JPEG-heavy docx went 3x cheaper (409 s -> 138 s expected for 20 MB of JPEG).** Neither corpus carries JPEG,
  the container's JPEG rate rests on one album, and the one JPEG-heavy B5 corpus (`.pptx`) ran 1.41x over it. So
  the lift is by PNG share, and a docx with little PNG keeps the chain rate (the higher, safer one).
- **My first version also moved Extreme.** B5 measured `.docx` at Extreme; a test that compared files with and
  without counts could not see it. The lift is now Insane-only, and a test compares a docx with an `.xlsx` of
  the same counts at Normal and Extreme (they must be identical). That test fails on the first version.
- Per-file, on the real corpora: 8 of 20 and 19 of 55 files are priced by counts; the totals barely move
  (4,704 s and 2,272 s expected) because the PNG-dominant files carry the time.

**Not lifted, still one corpus each:** `.xlsx` (PNG 6.1 MB in 7.2 MB, container 3.4x over), `.pptx` (24 of 40 MB
JPEG, 1.64x chain, 1.41x container), `.zip`, `.jar`, `.apk`. The size ratio and the planner's piping for `.docx`
are unchanged (B5's measured ones).
