# B17 — Insane moves to zpaqfranz `-m58`

**2026-09-26/27**, this laptop (2-core i7-3540M, 16 GB, Windows 10 Pro 19045),
zpaqfranz **v64.8j**, on AC power with the High Performance power scheme.

B12's third round found zpaqfranz `-m5` 7.26% smaller than the `-m4` Insane
shipped, on deflate-expanded data, and filed the question as B17 with three
options: keep `-m4`, move to `-m5`, or offer `-m5` as a choice. The owner's
answer was **"whatever is best for compression"**. So this round asked one
more question before choosing: does anything beat plain `-m5`?

It does. zpaq's method number takes a block-size digit, and bigger blocks
compress better. **Insane now runs `-m58`**: method 5 with 256 MiB blocks.

---

## 1. The probe — block size, on the 237 MB deflate `.pcf`

Same intermediate as B12 round 3 (`fresh.pcf`, 236,958,376 B, SHA-256
`c0fc71cb…c0984`). Every archive restored and SHA-256 compared. zpaqfranz
echoes plain `-m5` as `-m56` (2^6 = 64 MiB blocks), `-m57` as 128 MiB and
`-m58` as 256 MiB; confirmed with `-verbose` on a smoke file.

| setting | blocks | archive | vs `-m4` | vs `-m5` | compress | restore | peak RAM (c / x) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `-m4 -t2` (Insane before B17) | 4 × 64 MiB | 46,575,605 | — | +7.83% | 168.4 s | 186.2 s | 915 / 786 MiB |
| `-m5 -t2` | 4 × 64 MiB | 43,194,463 | −7.26% | — | 1,070.4 s † | 986.5–1,076.7 s | 1,819 / 1,691 MiB |
| `-m57 -t2` | 2 × 128 MiB | 42,933,026 | −7.82% | −0.61% | 1,176.3 s | 899.8 s | 2,903 / 2,881 MiB |
| **`-m58 -t2`** | **1 × 256 MiB** | **42,852,397** | **−7.99%** | **−0.79%** | 1,263.8 s | 1,322.8 s | 2,761 / 2,707 MiB |

† The only timed `-m5` compress on this corpus, from 2026-09-26. The first
`-m5` run, on 2026-09-23, was not timed: its file timestamps put it at about
541 s. That's not reconciled (see §4).

**Diminishing, but real:** 64 → 128 MiB blocks took 0.61% off, and 128 →
256 MiB took another 0.19%. `-m58` is the smallest of everything measured in
B12 and B17: 14.7% smaller than LOLZ and 17.3% smaller than Extreme's
`precomp → 7z` on this corpus.

**Why block size matters only for big jobs.** zpaq compresses each block
independently, starting a fresh model. An input smaller than 64 MiB is a
single block whatever the setting, so a small job gets the same archive at
`-m58` as at `-m5`. The gain comes from the model carrying context across
more data, which is exactly the regime Insane's overnight jobs are in. The
62.5 MB installer `.pcf` from B12 is one block at every size, which is why it
wasn't probed.

## 2. Why stop at 256 MiB: memory

RAM grows with the block. At the default thread count (zpaqfranz uses
physical cores, 2 here), two blocks are in flight on any input of 512 MB or
more. So the number that decides is the two-thread peak:

| | one busy thread | two busy threads |
|---|---:|---:|
| `-m5` (64 MiB) | 895 MiB | 1,819 MiB |
| `-m57` (128 MiB) | — | 2,903 MiB |
| **`-m58` (256 MiB)** | **2,761 MiB** | **4,916 MiB** |

The `-m58` two-thread figure comes from a 645 MB tar of three distinct
installers copied from the Programs corpus (`vagrant_2.4.2_windows_amd64.msi`,
`phprunner-setup.exe`, `AxureRP-Setup.exe`): three blocks, two in flight. The
peak was read from the live process's `PeakWorkingSet64` every half second
until it exited: **5,154,344,960 B**, after 4,237 s of compress. The restore
that follows was stopped deliberately, because only the RAM was wanted. That
archive's size isn't claimed anywhere.

**4,916 MiB is inside the budget** the RAM module already holds the machine to
(`_RAM_BUDGET = 0.55` of 16 GB, 8.8 GiB). `-m59` (512 MiB blocks) could not be
measured: a 237 MB input is one block at 256 or 512 MiB alike, so it would
need an input of 1 GB or more to show anything. It was not pursued.

**-m58's second thread costs less than its first.** Two busy threads peaked
at 4,916 MiB, not 2 × 2,761 = 5,521. About 606 MiB of the footprint is
shared. `excmp/ram.py` now models each method as *shared + per busy thread*.

## 3. What the change exposed: the RAM cell was never zpaqfranz's

The comparison table's peak-RAM column has always computed Insane's figure
with **7-Zip's LZMA2 formula**. `_ram_words` knew only Fast and "everything
else". For a Silesia-sized job at the default thread count that printed
**7,084 MiB**, a number with no relation to the stage that runs. It now
follows the chain. When Insane really ends in zpaqfranz, it quotes the
measured zpaqfranz model at physical-core count. With zpaqfranz missing,
Insane falls back to 7-Zip, and LZMA2's figure is right again.

The block count depends on what zpaqfranz receives, which is Precomp's output
of the *piped* files only, and can be several times their size. So the cell
takes the piped bytes (stored files never reach zpaqfranz) and inflates them
by `planner.precomp_inflation` before counting blocks. That can over-read a
job whose deflate streams turn out not to exist, and it never under-reads.
Over-reading is the direction the rest of the RAM column already takes.

### ...and rendering it caught the first version of the fix

`tools/qa_sweep.py` showed the Insane row quoting **2.7 GB of RAM for its
371.5 KB demo job**. The first model charged every job a full 256 MiB block.
zpaq sizes its model to the block's actual size, and nothing in the suite
looked at the number. Measured on slices of the installer `.pcf`, `-m58 -t2`,
each restored and SHA-256 compared:

| input | peak RAM |
|---:|---:|
| 1 MiB | 99.4 MiB |
| 4 MiB | 144.8 MiB |
| 16 MiB | 313.5 MiB |
| 59.6 MiB (the whole `.pcf`) | 895 MiB |

That's about 85 MiB plus 14.3 bytes per byte in flight, fitted through the
1 and 16 MiB points. The 4 MiB point sits 1.8% above the line, and the whole
`.pcf` 4.5% below it, out of sample. The line applies until it meets the
full-block figures in §2. The demo job now shows about 110 MiB. This is the
same lesson `effective_dict` taught the LZMA2 figure, when a 371 KB job was
once advertised 6 GB. (A planned 64 MiB slice turned out to be the whole
62.5 MB file, and its run was cut by the harness's own timeout during the
restore. The full run of that input is the 895 MiB row.)

## 3b. What the adversarial review found

Three reviewers on different lenses went over the branch diff, and a
skeptic tried to refute each finding. These were reproduced and fixed, each
with a failing test first:

- **The RAM justification was not enforced.** `-m58` was chosen because its
  *two-thread* peak fits the budget, but the Worker threads setting went
  straight through as `-t`, and with no `-t` zpaqfranz takes every physical
  core. Eight threads would need about 17 GB. `ram.zpaqfranz_threads` now caps
  the count at what fits `_RAM_BUDGET` (3 on this 16 GB laptop; the default
  stays at 2 physical cores), and the stage always passes it explicitly, for
  compress and restore.
- **`--low-memory` extraction never reached zpaqfranz.** G7's
  `extract_threads=1` was ignored by `build_extract_cmd`. At `-m58` each
  restoring thread holds about 2.7 GiB, so that mattered.
- **A pre-B17 calibration entry would override the measured rate.** J7 keys
  learned rates by chain, and `precomp+zpaqfranz` changed meaning from `-m4`
  to `-m58`. Entries are now stamped with the zpaq method, and unstamped ones
  are dropped on load.
- **A failed restore left its scratch folder** in `ctx.temp_dir`, outside
  every job directory's cleanup. At `-m58` that can be most of a `.pcf`.

A second pass of the same review, on the fixed tree, confirmed four more.
All are fixed, and all were checked in the rendered GUI:

- **Stored bytes were billed at the chain's restore rate.** `estimate_time`
  charged every byte at the chain's verify rate. At Insane's measured
  ~0.1 MB/s, a 2 GB stored video beside 20 MB of text came out at **over four
  hours**. Stored files are restored by a zip copy, so they now cost
  `io_rate`: that job is now estimated at 447 s. B17 caused this. The old
  15 MB/s fallback had hidden the same flaw at about two minutes. The
  estimator backtest still passes.
- **The RAM cell showed only the worst case**, with Precomp inflating the
  input about 5×. That's 4.8 GB for Silesia, where zpaqfranz actually got one
  block (2.7 GB). The cell now keeps the conservative figure on its first
  line and puts "Precomp finds nothing – worst case" on its second.
- **The inflation factor was averaged over stored files too**, so adding a
  stored video lowered zpaqfranz's quoted RAM. It is now computed over that
  profile's piped files only.
- **The cell assumed the default thread count** while the stage used the
  Worker threads setting. The setting now reaches the cell through
  `profile_comparison(zpaq_threads=…)`, so both call `zpaqfranz_threads`
  with the same request.

**Found and not fixed here, because it is older than B17 and touches every
stage:** `run_tool` checks for Cancel only when the tool prints a line. The
end-to-end runs show zpaqfranz printing no percentage for almost the whole
of a restore, so Cancel and window-close may not respond for the length of
an Insane D9 pass. Filed as K6. First step: capture what zpaqfranz actually
prints during `x`, and when.

## 4. Two timing findings, one unexplained

- **Method-5 context mixing barely speeds up with a second thread on this
  laptop.** `-m57` with two blocks on two threads took 1,176 s. `-m58` with
  one block on one thread took 1,264 s. The two-thread `-m58` run kept about
  1.5 cores busy (3,582 s of CPU in its first 2,338 s) and still averaged only
  0.15 MB/s. At `-m4`, a second thread bought 1.43× on 2026-09-23 (241 s →
  168 s). The likely reason is memory bandwidth (larger CM tables on two
  threads, one memory bus), but it's a reading of the numbers, not a
  measurement of the cause.
- **The 2026-09-23 `-m5` compress, at about 541 s from file timestamps,
  doesn't fit.** A speed check on 2026-09-27, `-m4 -t2` on the installer
  `.pcf`, took 122.9 s against 09-23's median of 119.0 s (spread 115.1–134.9).
  So the machine isn't slower today. Every timed method-5 run since has
  taken 1,070–1,264 s on this corpus. The 541 s figure was never timed, and it
  is treated here as the outlier, not the baseline.

## 5. End to end, through `engine.compress`

The probe measures the codec. This measures the product: `Profile.INSANE`
through `engine.compress`, the path the app and the CLI take. That's tar →
Precomp → zpaqfranz, then the D9 gate's full restore and SHA-256 check
against the ledger, then a separate `engine.extract` timed on its own.
Harness: `b17_e2e.py`.

| corpus | method | original | archive | saved | compress (incl. D9) | extract | verified |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Silesia, 12 files | **`-m58`** | 211,938,580 | **37,542,028** | **82.29%** | 3,810.1 s | 1,555.7 s | 12/12 |
| Silesia (B8, 2026-09-08) | `-m4` | 211,938,580 | 41,216,918 | 80.55% | 16.7 min | — | 12/12 |
| installer sample, 13 files | **`-m58`** | 55,733,142 | **45,951,032** | **17.55%** | 744.6 s | 378.1 s | 13/13 |
| installer sample, 13 files | `-m4` | 55,733,142 | 46,223,108 | 17.06% | 299.3 s ‡ | 193.8 s ‡ | 13/13 |

**On Silesia, the archive is 8.92% smaller than B8's `-m4` run**, and the
wait is 3.8× longer: 63.5 minutes against 16.7. The installer sample is the
same 13 files `excmp.sample.pick_sample` chose from the Programs corpus for
B12. On it, `-m58` is **0.59% smaller than `-m4`** (272,076 B), the same margin
the bare `.pcf` showed (0.60%). That's expected: a 62.5 MB `.pcf` is one
block at either size, so this is `-m5`'s gain, and the block size adds nothing.

‡ The `-m4` installer run overlapped the full test suite, which started
while it ran. Its times are an upper bound, and the real `-m58`/`-m4` time
multiple on that sample is larger than the 2.5× these rows show.

### The estimator had never measured Insane's speed

`_CODEC_RATE` and `_VERIFY_RATE` had no `precomp+zpaqfranz` key, so Insane's
time came from the 2.5 MB/s and 15 MB/s fallbacks. For the Silesia job above
that meant a predicted **41–285 s (expected 99 s) against 3,810 s measured**.
The fallback was wrong even at `-m4`, against B8's 16.7 minutes. B17 adds the
keys from these two runs, using the estimator's own definitions:

| | Silesia (Precomp found nothing) | installers (Precomp working) | shipped |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| codec rate: piped bytes ÷ (compress − extract) | 0.094 MB/s | 0.087 MB/s | **0.091 MB/s** (geo. mean) |
| verify rate: piped bytes ÷ extract | 0.136 MB/s | 0.0845 MB/s | **0.107 MB/s** |

The codec rates agree within 8%. The verify rate is taken over *piped* bytes
because the review (§3b) found stored bytes being billed at the chain's
restore rate. They are restored by a zip copy, so they now cost `io_rate`.
On piped bytes the installer run is slower (its 23.8 MB stored share had
flattered the first figure, 0.147 MB/s), hence the wider pair. With these,
the Silesia estimate is **2,709–9,434 s, expected 4,310 s**, against the
3,810 s measured.

The size slope is re-anchored the way B8 anchored every chain: through
Silesia and the physical (1.0, 1.0) point. The Silesia probe is 0.4047
(bytes-weighted `sample_ratio_max`, re-measured by this run and identical to
B8's), the ratio 0.1771, so the factor is 0.4377 and **k = 0.945** (0.873 at
`-m4`). The installer run is the cross-check, not a fit: the estimator's range
for it is 31,603,834–49,623,215 with 46,324,391 expected, and the archive
came out at 45,951,032, **0.8% under expected**. Both checks are now tests.

### The progress bar

`gui/progress.py` weighted zpaqfranz at 50 against Precomp's 35, so the bar
crossed half way while zpaqfranz had barely begun. Measured with bare timings,
zpaqfranz is 90–93% of an Insane job's compress time on every corpus:
1,264 s against Precomp's 141 s on the deflate zip, about 360 s against 9 s
on the installer sample, and about 2,050 s against 3 s on Silesia. So the
weight is now 400.

Two things this run showed about progress that B17 does not fix (filed as
I11):

- **The bar holds at 95% through the whole D9 restore.** `ChainProgress`
  keeps its peak, and the restore reports through the same stage ids. At
  `-m58` that restore is about 40% of the job: 1,556 s of 3,810 on Silesia.
- **zpaqfranz prints no restore progress until it's nearly done.** In both
  runs its extract reported its first percentage 52 s (Silesia) and 3 s
  (installers) before finishing. That's also why the per-stage timings in
  `b17-e2e.jsonl`'s `transitions` can't be used as stage durations.


### Two layout guards moved, and why that's the honest outcome

With Insane's time measured instead of borrowed from a 2.5 MB/s fallback,
the chooser's "not worth it" logic now fires on the suite's populated
fixture: Insane is ~24× slower than Extreme for the same result there. The
Insane row gains a one-line note, and the table's caption gains a pointer to
it. `test_gui_compact` (range rows ≤ 56 px) saw the Insane row at 69 px, and
`test_gui_fit` (populated page ≤ 447 px) saw 504 px.

Measured both ways before moving anything: with that note suppressed, the
branch's page is **exactly 447 px, identical to `main`**, so every pixel of
the growth is the warning (row +19 px, caption +38 px). Both guards were
re-baselined to the exact measured heights with this written beside them,
so any *further* growth still trips them. B8 set the precedent when honest
estimate ranges added a line: hiding a true warning to protect a budget was
rejected then and is rejected now.

## 6. Reproducing it

Scripts and raw rows are in `C:\excmp-bench\b17\`:
`b17_blocks.py` (the probe), `b17_ram2.py` (speed check and the two-thread
RAM input), `b17_e2e.py` (the end-to-end runs through `engine.compress`), and
`b17-results.jsonl` / `b17-e2e.jsonl`. They import `b12z_run.py` from the
same folder, which reads its `.pcf` inputs from `C:\excmp-bench\b12\`.

```
zpaqfranz.exe a <out>.zpaq <in> -m58 -longpath -t2
zpaqfranz.exe x <out>.zpaq -to <dir> -longpath
```
